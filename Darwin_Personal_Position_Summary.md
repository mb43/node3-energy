## The training

You're enrolling in a private fast-track electrician programme — the same one Matt is doing. Around 12–18 months of intensive study covering Level 2/3 Electrical Installations, 18th Edition wiring regulations, NVQ Level 3, and the AM2 practical assessment. The end result is a JIB ECS Gold Card — the industry standard credential that DNOs, grid operators, and contractors all recognise.

Why private rather than an apprenticeship? An apprenticeship takes 3.5 years and pays very little. This gets you to the same qualification in 12–18 months. You're qualified and earning properly by early 2028 instead of late 2029. That matters because the business needs qualified in-house electricians during its growth phase — not after it.

Cost: £9,000. Every node Dovecote commissions in-house saves ~£2,000 in external contractor costs. At 10 nodes, that's £20,000 saved — more than double the training cost.

## The cons and risks — honestly

- **Personal liability.** This is a personal loan, not a company loan. If Dovecote fails and you can't repay, you personally owe £25,000 (plus interest). The business plan is solid, but you're 18 and this is real money.

- **UC minimum income floor from April 2027.** After your 12-month startup period, UC will assume you're earning at least ~£693/mo regardless of actual income. If wages aren't being paid reliably at that point, UC won't make up the shortfall — it'll assess you as if you're earning MIF anyway.

- **Training isn't guaranteed to finish on time.** Fast-track programmes can slip. If your JIB Gold Card takes 20 months instead of 12–18, external electricians are needed for longer and cost savings are delayed.

- **Housing element may be lower than expected.** Under-35 rule means you get the Shared Accommodation Rate, not a full 1-bed LHA rate. Verify the exact Southampton SAR figure before banking on ~£400.

- **You're 18 and co-director of a company with your father.** A significant personal and financial commitment. The loan is yours either way.
